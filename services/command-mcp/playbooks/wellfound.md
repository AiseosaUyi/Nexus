# Wellfound — playbook (kind: inbound)

Startup job board (ex-AngelList Talent). **No vetting gate** — you build a profile and apply
directly, so this is the fastest platform on the list to go live on. Roles skew startup:
contract, part-time and full-time mixed together. Aise's Web3/fintech background is squarely
the audience.

Rated 5/5 region-friendly, medium difficulty, very high potential.

## Setup (one-off, before any run)
- Sign up with LinkedIn — pre-fills history and is the fastest route. **Aise does the signup.**
- Attach a résumé PDF. Wellfound prioritises profiles with a résumé in recruiter search, so this
  is not optional decoration.
- Headline should match the Contra one: `Senior Product Designer & Next.js Developer | SaaS, Fintech`.
- Set location preference to **Remote**, and flag US-hours overlap in the profile text — that
  overlap is the thing startups screen for on international hires.

## Read (each run)
- Messages / recruiter outreach.
- Saved searches for `Product Designer`, `Design Engineer`, `Front-end Designer`, remote + contract.
- Application statuses that moved.

## Draft
- Applications: Wellfound notes are read like a cover note, not a form. Two or three sentences,
  their specific problem, one proof point with a number (1.5M users, $10.4M seed), one link.
- Capture as `type=job`, job text in raw_text.
- Score fit hard: funded stage, design-led team, remote-friendly, contract or fractional.

## What good looks like here
- Profile reads as an operator, not a jobseeker: shipped outcomes, not responsibilities.
- Both skills visible — design *and* front-end. That combination is the differentiator on a
  board where most designers cannot ship code.

## Health inputs
- Profile completeness, résumé attached, application→reply rate, recency of activity.
- Top fix early on is almost always: attach the résumé, then tighten the headline.

## Execute (after approval)
- Open the role → Apply → paste approved note → submit → `cc_mark_sent`.
- Never accept an offer or agree a rate on Aise's behalf.
